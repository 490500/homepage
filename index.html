The Mastery of Modern Foil Fencing

Technique, Tactics, Strategy, and Science — A World-Class Comprehensive Curriculum
How to Use This Book

This book follows the practical curriculum structure, organized into Parts I–VIII and Chapters 1–21. Each chapter retains its practical combat sections and, where the content naturally leads to a scientific discipline, includes a Subject Section. Four core disciplines are integrated:

  • MP Mathematical Physics
  • BM Biomechanics
  • NS Neuroscience
  • SS Sports Science

Other disciplines (psychophysiology, data science, measurement, coaching science, nutrition, sleep, environmental physiology, growth and development, etc.) are mentioned as needed within relevant tactical sections and are not listed as separate subject sections.

The following subject sections arise from the natural extension of the chapter content, rather than being forced in to fulfill a "one per chapter" requirement:

  • 2.3 Motor Learning — Terminology learning and pre-bout rituals naturally involve motor learning and arousal regulation.
  • 3.3 Statics — The en garde stance is fundamentally a problem of statics.
  • 4.4 Energy Cost — Footwork training inevitably involves load and energy.
  • 5.4 / 5.5 Mechanics + Explosive Power — The lunge and flèche are the core of mechanics and energy systems.
  • 6.5 Optimal Stopping — Attack timing is essentially an optimal stopping problem.
  • 7.4 Force & Friction — Blade engagement is essentially mechanical interaction.
  • 8.3 Motor Inhibition — Continuation attacks involve inhibition and re-initiation.
  • 9.5 Leverage Mechanics — Parrying is essentially a lever system.
  • 10.5 Reaction Time — Ripostes are essentially a reaction-time problem.
  • 11.6 Stochastic Processes — Counter-attack timing is essentially a stochastic process.
  • 12.4 / 12.5 Game Theory + Neuroscience — Deception is essentially game theory and perception.
  • 13.5 Sequence Learning — Compound techniques are essentially motor sequences.
  • 14.4 Asymmetric Mechanics — Left-handed vs. right-handed bouts are essentially a geometric problem.
  • 15.5 Momentum & Path — Neutralizing attacks is essentially momentum management.
  • 16.5 Bayesian Decision — Style matchups are essentially decision problems.
  • 17.3 Reach Mechanics — Physical profile differences are essentially mechanical differences.
  • 18.6 / 18.7 Dynamic Programming + Pressure — Bout management is essentially decision-making and pressure.
  • 19.6 Referee Perception — Refereeing is essentially perception and decision-making.
  • 20.3 Energy Metabolism — Physical conditioning is essentially the energy system.
  • 21.4 Material Mechanics — Equipment is essentially material mechanics.
